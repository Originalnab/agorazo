# 1. Executive Summary

Agorazo is a technology-enabled automotive marketplace designed to make buying, selling, importing, paying for, clearing and registering vehicles in Ghana simpler, more transparent and more trustworthy.

The platform will initially operate as a controlled vehicle sales and import platform where Agorazo lists vehicles supplied through an established China-based vehicle supplier who already sources and ships vehicles to Tema Port.

The Chinese supplier will provide Agorazo with a price covering the vehicle and shipment to Tema Port. Agorazo will add its own commercial markup and offer the vehicle to Ghanaian customers through the Agorazo platform.

Customers will have the option to:

- Purchase a vehicle delivered to Tema Port and arrange their own clearing.
- Purchase the vehicle and allow Agorazo to coordinate clearing.
- Add DVLA registration.
- Add insurance.
- Add vehicle delivery.
- Choose available staged or milestone payment arrangements.

Over time, Agorazo will evolve into a multi-seller automotive marketplace where verified car dealerships and approved vehicle businesses can create seller accounts, subscribe to the platform, list vehicles, manage customers, create approved purchase-payment arrangements and receive payments.

Agorazo will therefore develop from an online dealership into a broader:

**Automotive Marketplace + Dealer Operating Platform + Payment Orchestration Platform + Automotive Services Ecosystem.**

Agorazo will initially operate without escrow. Payments will be processed using licensed payment service providers such as Paystack or Hubtel, with the platform recording customer payments, payment milestones, seller obligations, service-provider obligations and company revenue.

Bank of Ghana currently lists Paystack Ghana and Hubtel as licensed Enhanced Payment Service Providers.

A genuine escrow service may be introduced later through an appropriately regulated banking or financial partner.

# 3. The Problem

Purchasing vehicles in Ghana, particularly vehicles sourced internationally, can involve significant uncertainty.

Buyers frequently deal with several separate parties:

A dealer sells the vehicle.

Another person handles shipping.

Another person handles customs clearance.

Another person assists with registration.

Insurance is handled separately.

Payments may be made through multiple accounts.

Documents are often exchanged through WhatsApp.

Customers may not have a central place to track the entire transaction.

For imported vehicles, buyers may also struggle to clearly understand the distinction between:

- Vehicle cost
- Shipping cost
- Customs duty
- Port charges
- Clearing-agent charges
- Registration costs
- Insurance
- Dealer margin
- Delivery

Agorazo addresses this problem by bringing the commercial transaction and associated automotive services into one platform.

# 6. Vehicle Pricing Engine

One of Agorazo's key internal systems will be its configurable pricing and markup engine.

Agorazo should not rely on manually calculating the selling price of every vehicle.

The platform should support markup rules by vehicle type.

For example:

Small Sedan  
Default markup: 12%

Mid-size Sedan  
Default markup: 10%

SUV  
Default markup: 9%

EV  
Default markup: 8%

Luxury Vehicle  
Default markup: 7%

Pickup  
Default markup: fixed GHS amount

Commercial Vehicle  
Custom markup

Agorazo administrators should also be able to override the default markup for a particular vehicle.

The pricing hierarchy should therefore be:

**Specific Vehicle Markup**

If none exists:

↓

**Vehicle Category Markup**

If none exists:

↓

**Global Default Markup**

This allows Agorazo to automatically convert supplier prices into customer prices.

# 7. Separate Pricing for Vehicle Services

Agorazo should treat each service as an independent profit centre.

The system should never combine everything into one undefined margin.

The principal pricing components will be:

### Vehicle

Supplier Tema cost  
+ Agorazo vehicle markup  
= Vehicle selling price

### Customs

Official customs assessment

This should not be represented as Agorazo revenue.

### Clearing

Clearing-agent cost  
+ Agorazo clearing-service markup  
= Customer clearing-service price

### DVLA

Actual registration/inspection expenses  
+ Agorazo processing fee  
= Customer DVLA package price

### Insurance

Insurance-provider quotation  
+ authorised referral/commission arrangement where applicable

### Delivery

Logistics cost  
+ Agorazo delivery/service margin

This gives management visibility into exactly where the company earns money.

# 10. Customs and Clearing

Agorazo should partner with a properly qualified/licensed customs clearing business rather than depend solely on an informal individual.

GRA states that vehicle import clearing involves documentation such as the Bill of Lading/Waybill and title, while vehicle duty estimates can be checked through ICUMS using information including VIN/chassis number, make, model and year.

The platform should maintain two customs values:

**Estimated customs amount**

and

**Final ICUMS assessment**

This is important because the amount displayed before customs valuation may not equal the final government assessment.

The customer's portal could show:

Estimated customs:

GHS 55,000

Final customs assessment:

GHS 57,430

Difference:

GHS 2,430

Agorazo's terms should explain how any difference is handled.

GRA's published import procedure confirms that customs declarations are processed in ICUMS through classification, valuation and approval before duties are paid using the generated tax bill.

Agorazo should therefore keep:

**Government customs charges**

separate from:

**Agorazo clearing-service charges.**

# 12. DVLA Services

Customers may choose to handle registration themselves or purchase a DVLA processing package from Agorazo.

DVLA currently lists requirements for vehicle registration including the vehicle itself, original customs entry documents, an inspection report from a designated Private Vehicle Testing Station and valid identification. An authorised agent can act with appropriate authorisation.

Agorazo can therefore build a structured registration service around:

- Vehicle inspection coordination
- Registration documentation
- Number plate processing
- Customer authorisation
- Status tracking
- Final document delivery

Official DVLA-related charges should remain distinct from Agorazo's service fee.

# 13. Payments

Agorazo will initially operate without escrow.

Payments may be accepted through licensed providers such as:

**Paystack**

or

**Hubtel**

with bank transfer and mobile money forming important Ghana-specific payment channels.

Paystack supports Ghana mobile-money payments through its API.

Paystack also supports transfers to recipient accounts, including Ghana mobile-money recipients, as part of its payout infrastructure.

Agorazo's software should maintain a transaction ledger even though the PSP/bank controls the actual movement of money.

# 16. Payment Splitting and Seller Settlement

As Agorazo becomes a marketplace, the platform can use PSP marketplace capabilities where commercially approved.

Paystack's multi-split infrastructure allows a payment to be divided between a merchant's payout account and one or more subaccounts using flat or percentage-based allocation rules.

Paystack specifically describes transaction splits as applicable to marketplace situations where a vendor and platform owner receive their respective portions of a payment.

This can eventually support transactions such as:

Customer pays:

GHS 300,000

Seller entitlement:

GHS 282,000

Agorazo transaction/service fee:

GHS 18,000

However, Agorazo should still obtain commercial approval from its PSP for its transaction sizes and business model before launch.

# 17. International Supplier Payments

The platform therefore records:

Supplier payment approved

↓

# 32. Company Structure

For a marketplace with multiple commercial partners, payments and potentially future investment, Agorazo should generally be established through an appropriate incorporated company structure rather than operated informally.

The Office of the Registrar of Companies currently administers companies incorporated under Ghana's Companies Act 2019, including private companies limited by shares.

Ghana's Companies Regulations also specify incorporation documentation for private companies limited by shares, including director, company-secretary and beneficial-ownership documentation.

The final corporate/shareholding structure should be established with legal and accounting advice.
